1. PURPOSE & PHILOSOPHY
────────────────────────
NexusTech Solutions believes that performance management is not an annual event — it is
a continuous, human-centered process. The Performance Review Process exists to:
  - Give every employee clear, actionable feedback on their contributions
  - Align individual goals with company OKRs
  - Recognize and reward high performance fairly and transparently
  - Identify growth areas and provide structured development support
  - Inform compensation decisions, promotions, and role changes

We follow a "no surprises" principle: nothing in a formal performance review should be
a surprise. Managers are expected to deliver real-time feedback throughout the year so
that formal reviews are a summary — not a revelation.

2. REVIEW CYCLES
─────────────────

NexusTech operates a dual-cycle performance review model:

  MID-YEAR CHECK-IN (July)
    - Informal, forward-looking conversation between employee and manager
    - No formal rating; goal is to course-correct and re-energize H2 goals
    - Completed via Workday check-in form
    - Estimated time: 30–45 minutes 1:1 conversation

  ANNUAL PERFORMANCE REVIEW (December–January)
    - Formal review covering the full calendar year (Jan 1 – Dec 31)
    - Includes self-assessment, manager assessment, and peer feedback (360°)
    - Results in a performance rating that informs compensation and promotion decisions
    - Timeline:
        Phase 1 — Self-Assessment Open       : December 1
        Phase 2 — Peer Nominations Due        : December 5
        Phase 3 — Peer Feedback Deadline      : December 15
        Phase 4 — Manager Assessment Deadline : January 5
        Phase 5 — Calibration Sessions        : January 6–15
        Phase 6 — Results Communicated        : January 20–31
        Phase 7 — Compensation Letters Sent   : February 1–7

  PROBATIONARY REVIEW (30/60/90 Day — New Hires Only)
    - 30-day check-in: Culture fit, onboarding progress, early concerns
    - 60-day check-in: Role clarity, initial productivity, relationship building
    - 90-day formal review: Formal pass/extend probation/end employment decision
    - Conducted by direct manager; HR Business Partner joins the 90-day review

3. PERFORMANCE RATING SCALE
─────────────────────────────
NexusTech uses a 5-level rating scale:

  Rating 5 — EXCEPTIONAL
    Definition  : Consistently exceeds all goals; delivers results that create outsized
                  impact well beyond the scope of the role; serves as a role model.
    Expectation : Rare — approximately 5–10% of the population
    Implication : Strong promotion case; top-of-band merit increase; spot bonus eligible

  Rating 4 — EXCEEDS EXPECTATIONS
    Definition  : Consistently meets all goals and frequently exceeds them; takes on
                  additional scope; demonstrates impact beyond role definition.
    Expectation : ~20–25% of population
    Implication : Above-median merit increase; promotion consideration

  Rating 3 — MEETS EXPECTATIONS
    Definition  : Consistently delivers on all core responsibilities and goals;
                  reliable, effective, and valuable team member.
    Expectation : ~50–60% of population (the "strong performer" baseline)
    Implication : Standard merit increase; solid standing

  Rating 2 — PARTIALLY MEETS EXPECTATIONS
    Definition  : Inconsistently meets goals; some areas of strong performance offset
                  by gaps; may be new to role or navigating significant challenges.
    Expectation : ~10–15% of population
    Implication : Below-median or no merit increase; Performance Improvement Plan (PIP)
                  discussion initiated; close manager support provided

  Rating 1 — DOES NOT MEET EXPECTATIONS
    Definition  : Consistently fails to meet core responsibilities; despite coaching
                  and support, performance remains below acceptable threshold.
    Expectation : < 5% of population
    Implication : Formal PIP required; no merit increase; employment at risk

  Note: Distribution guidance is not a forced ranking. Managers evaluate each employee
  independently. The distribution is a calibration check, not a quota.

4. GOAL SETTING (OKRs)
───────────────────────

4.1 Framework
NexusTech uses the OKR (Objectives and Key Results) framework company-wide.

  Objective  : Qualitative, inspiring, direction-setting statement
  Key Result : Quantitative, measurable outcome that signals the objective is achieved
               (2–5 Key Results per Objective)

Example OKR (Engineering):
  Objective  : Deliver a best-in-class API platform that developers love
  Key Results:
    KR1 — Reduce average API response time from 320ms to under 150ms by Q3
    KR2 — Achieve 99.95% API uptime across all production endpoints (up from 99.8%)
    KR3 — Increase API developer NPS from 42 to 65 as measured by Q4 survey
    KR4 — Launch v2 API with versioning support and deprecation policy by October 31

4.2 Goal-Setting Timeline
  - Company OKRs published by leadership: First week of January
  - Team/Squad OKRs finalized: End of January
  - Individual OKRs agreed upon by manager and employee: End of February
  - Q2 OKR refresh (minor adjustments allowed): April 1–15
  - Mid-year OKR retrospective: July check-in

4.3 Goal Categories
  Every employee must have goals in the following categories:
    a) Business/Functional Goals (50–60% weight) — delivery of core role responsibilities
    b) Behavioral/Values Goals (20–30% weight) — how work is done (NexusTech values)
    c) Development Goals (10–20% weight) — skills, certifications, learning targets

5. SELF-ASSESSMENT
───────────────────
Each employee completes a self-assessment in Workday covering:

  Section 1: Goal Achievement
    - Rate your own achievement on each OKR (% complete, narrative explanation)
    - Highlight 2–3 most impactful accomplishments with measurable outcomes
    - Reflect on any goals missed and explain contributing factors

  Section 2: NexusTech Values Reflection
    NexusTech's 5 core values (rated 1–5 with written evidence):
      ★ Customer Obsession  — How did you put customers first?
      ★ Move with Urgency   — How did you bias toward action and speed?
      ★ Own It              — Where did you take accountability?
      ★ Think Big           — How did you challenge the status quo?
      ★ One Team            — How did you contribute beyond your immediate role?

  Section 3: Development Reflection
    - Progress on learning/development goals
    - Skills gained or certifications earned
    - Areas where you want to grow in the coming year

  Section 4: Upward Feedback
    - Confidential section for feedback on your manager (separate from review; fed to
      manager's own review by their skip-level manager)

  Recommended length: 500–1,000 words total. Avoid bullet lists — write in narrative form.

6. PEER FEEDBACK (360°)
────────────────────────

6.1 Process
  - Each employee nominates 3–5 peers for feedback (direct collaborators, cross-functional
    partners, or stakeholders outside the team)
  - Manager approves or modifies the peer list (must include at least 1 cross-functional peer)
  - HR adds any mandatory reviewers (e.g., key project stakeholders)
  - Peer feedback is anonymous in the employee-facing report; managers see attribution

6.2 Peer Feedback Questions (Workday form)
  - What has [Name] done especially well this year? (Required, 100–300 words)
  - What is one area where [Name] could have greater impact? (Required, 100–300 words)
  - Would you choose to work with [Name] again on a high-stakes project? (Yes/Somewhat/No + reason)
  - Rate [Name] on: Collaboration, Communication, Technical Quality, Reliability (1–5 scale)

6.3 Confidentiality
  - Peer feedback comments are aggregated and lightly paraphrased if fewer than 3 peers respond.
  - Managers must not reveal the identity of individual peer reviewers without consent.

7. MANAGER ASSESSMENT
──────────────────────
Managers complete the following in Workday:

  Section 1: Overall Performance Narrative (300–600 words)
    - Summary of the employee's year, key contributions, and growth

  Section 2: Goal Ratings
    - Rate each OKR: Exceeded / Met / Partially Met / Not Met
    - Written justification for each rating

  Section 3: Values Ratings
    - Rate each of the 5 NexusTech values (1–5) with behavioral examples

  Section 4: Overall Rating
    - Select rating (1–5) with written rationale
    - Flag if nomination for promotion, PIP, or special recognition applies

  Section 5: Development Plan for Next Year
    - 2–3 recommended focus areas with suggested actions (courses, stretch assignments, mentoring)

  Manager assessments are reviewed by the skip-level manager before calibration.

8. CALIBRATION SESSIONS
────────────────────────

8.1 Purpose
  Calibration ensures fairness and consistency across teams. It is where managers discuss
  and align on ratings across their organizations to reduce bias and grade inflation.

8.2 Structure
  Level 1 Calibration : Team-level (EM reviews ratings with their direct reports who manage people)
  Level 2 Calibration : Department-level (Director/VP reviews across teams)
  Level 3 Calibration : Executive Calibration (C-suite reviews for Directors and above)

8.3 Rules of Calibration
  - All proposed ratings and promotion nominations are discussed
  - Managers must provide evidence-based justifications for ratings
  - Distribution benchmarks are reviewed (not enforced as quotas)
  - Promotions require unanimous agreement from the calibration panel
  - Decisions made in calibration are final unless a formal appeal is filed (see Section 11)

9. DELIVERING THE REVIEW
─────────────────────────

9.1 The Review Conversation
  - Minimum 60 minutes scheduled; do not conduct via Slack or email
  - Private setting (video call with camera on for remote employees)
  - Suggested structure:
      [0–5 min]   Open — Set a positive, collaborative tone
      [5–20 min]  Employee shares self-assessment highlights
      [20–35 min] Manager shares assessment (start with strengths, then areas to grow)
      [35–50 min] Discuss development plan for next year; align on goals
      [50–60 min] Compensation conversation (if applicable) and Q&A
  - Never deliver a Rating 1 without HR Business Partner present

9.2 Best Practices for Managers
  ✓ Use specific examples, not generalizations ("In the Q3 platform migration, you...")
  ✓ Separate performance conversation from compensation (different conversations, different days)
  ✓ Do not read the written review verbatim — have a conversation
  ✓ Listen more than you talk (target 40% manager talking, 60% employee)
  ✗ Do not compare the employee to named colleagues ("Unlike Alex, you...")
  ✗ Do not raise new critical feedback for the first time in the formal review

10. PERFORMANCE IMPROVEMENT PLAN (PIP)
────────────────────────────────────────

10.1 When a PIP is Initiated
  A PIP is initiated when an employee receives a Rating 2 or 1 and informal coaching has
  not produced sufficient improvement. HR Business Partner must be involved.

10.2 PIP Structure
  Duration     : 30, 60, or 90 days (determined by HR based on severity)
  Contents:
    - Specific, measurable performance gaps
    - Clear, achievable success criteria for each gap
    - Resources and support provided (training, mentoring, workload adjustment)
    - Weekly check-in cadence with manager
    - Consequences if criteria not met (up to and including termination)

10.3 PIP Outcomes
  - Successful Completion   : PIP closed; employee returns to standard review cycle
  - Partial Improvement     : PIP extended or modified
  - Insufficient Improvement: Employment ended; HR and Legal involved

  Note: A PIP is not a precursor to termination — it is a genuine attempt at improvement.
  Approximately 40% of employees who enter a PIP at NexusTech complete it successfully.

11. APPEALS PROCESS
────────────────────
  - Employees who believe their rating is unfair may file an appeal within 10 business days
    of receiving their formal review
  - Appeals submitted to HR Business Partner via hr@nexustech.io
  - Appeal reviewed by skip-level manager + HR Business Partner within 15 business days
  - Appeal outcomes: Rating upheld / Rating adjusted / Additional calibration requested
  - If the employee remains unsatisfied, a final review by the CPO may be requested

12. PROMOTIONS
───────────────

12.1 Promotion Criteria
  Promotions at NexusTech are based on:
    a) Demonstrated performance at the next level (not just the current level) for 6+ months
    b) Business need / headcount availability at the next level
    c) Calibration panel approval
    d) No active PIP or disciplinary action

12.2 Promotion Timing
  - Promotions are effective February 1 (tied to annual review cycle)
  - Off-cycle promotions require CPO and CFO approval; reserved for exceptional cases

12.3 Levels & Titles (Engineering Example)
  IC Track:
    L3 — Software Engineer
    L4 — Senior Software Engineer
    L5 — Staff Software Engineer
    L6 — Principal Software Engineer
    L7 — Distinguished Engineer
    L8 — Fellow

  Management Track:
    M4 — Engineering Manager (manages 4–8 ICs)
    M5 — Senior Engineering Manager (manages EMs or 8–15 ICs)
    M6 — Director of Engineering
    M7 — Senior Director of Engineering
    M8 — VP of Engineering
